Hard limits

These are not preferences. Every exercise in this programme was chosen or modified to respect them.

Ankle — never enter end-range dorsiflexion

Your pain is at the front-outside of the ankle, only at end-range dorsiflexion under load, and the joint is not tender to touch. That pattern is compression of tissue at the front of the joint, so the treatment is to stay out of that range while loading everything around it.

  • No knee-to-wall stretching or calf stretching against a wall
  • No deep-squat ankle mobility drills
  • No heel-dropped calf raises off a step — every heel raise stops at flat foot
  • No squat or lunge below the depth where you feel the pinch; front heel stays elevated

Yes to: calf strength and endurance, evertor (peroneal) strength in mid-range, balance, hip control, and landing mechanics.

Elbow — no wide-grip pulling

Pain at the elbow crease with curls, reverse curls and wide-grip chin-ups points to the distal biceps tendon. That tendon does two jobs — it bends the elbow and it is your most powerful forearm supinator. A programme that only trains curls trains half of it.

  • No wide-grip chin-ups or pull-ups at any stage
  • No jumping straight to full-range curls — isometrics first, then slow eccentrics, then full range
  • No heavy reverse (pronated) curls until phase 3

Yes to: neutral or narrow grip pulling, loaded supination, and progressive eccentric loading.

What this programme is, and is not

This is a structured loading plan built around two suspected diagnoses that you described. It is not a diagnosis and it is not a substitute for assessment by a physiotherapist or sports doctor — particularly for the ankle, where repeated inversion sprains can leave behind bony changes, an osteochondral lesion or a loose body that need imaging rather than exercise. If pain is not trending down by the end of phase 1, get it looked at.

Three phases, twelve weeks

Move on when you meet the criteria, not when the calendar says so. Staying an extra week in a phase costs you nothing; skipping ahead costs you the whole programme.

Your week

Five sessions a week. Days are a suggestion — keep 48 hours between the two heavy elbow sessions, and never do two heavy ankle sessions back to back.

Running, AFL, snowboarding, kitesurfing

Phase 1: keep running only if it is currently pain-free during and the next morning — flat ground, no hills, no speed work. Hills and forefoot-heavy running drive dorsiflexion and are the most likely thing to flare the ankle. Kitesurfing loads the elbow hard through the bar; park it for phase 1.

Phase 2: rebuild running volume by no more than 10% a week. Add strides on flat ground late in the phase. Short kite sessions with a harness-heavy, arms-light technique once the elbow tolerates eccentric chin-ups.

Phase 3: add change of direction, then contested AFL work. Half-marathon-specific long runs belong here, not earlier. Snowboarding is mostly a mid-range ankle in a stiff boot, which is kind to you — but the landing and edge-catch risk is real, so it goes after the landing work is solid.

Progress log

Two numbers tell you whether this is working. Record them on the same day each week, at the same time of day, before training.

Single-leg heel raise — reps to failure

Your primary calf capacity measure. A healthy active adult should manage roughly 20–30 good reps. Side-to-side difference matters more than the absolute number — you want the injured side within 10% of the other.

Protocol: stand on flat floor, fingertips on a wall for balance only. Rise to full height, lower to flat foot — no heel drop below the toes. About 2 seconds per rep. Stop when your heel height drops or you need to push on the wall.

Modified for you: the demo is performed on the floor, which is what you want. Ignore any cue to lower past flat.

Knee-to-wall distance read the caution

You asked for this, so it is here — but it needs a caveat. Knee-to-wall is end-range dorsiflexion under load. It is the exact movement your hard limit rules out as an exercise. As a once-a-fortnight measurement it is useful; as a weekly habit it risks poking the thing you are trying to calm down.

How to take it safely: measure pain-limited, not maximum. Foot square to the wall, heel stays down, drive the knee forward and stop at the first hint of pinch — not past it. Record the distance in centimetres from the big toe to the wall at that point. Hold nothing; do not repeat it five times to "warm into it". That is a stretch, and stretching is off the menu.

What good looks like: the pain-free distance increasing week to week is the win — it means the joint is tolerating more range before it complains. Deliberately no video here: the standard demos all coach you to push into the end range.

About the videos

Every video ID on this page was checked three ways on 16 September 2026: against YouTube's oEmbed endpoint to confirm the video is live and public; against the watch page's playabilityStatus to catch videos that are members-only, private, age-gated or region-blocked; and by loading it through the YouTube IFrame Player API in a real browser to confirm it actually plays embedded. All 24 pass all three.

Two honest caveats

Videos need a connection. The page, your ticks and your log all work offline after the first load. The videos are hosted on YouTube, so they will not play without a network. The thumbnails are cached, so you will still see which exercise is which.

The members-only trap. The playability check exists because one video originally on this page — a banded supination isometric — turned out to be members-only. It passed both of the other checks: oEmbed returned a real title, and the player loaded fine, because a paywalled video still loads a working player, it just shows a join prompt instead of the footage. Only the watch-page status reveals it. It has been replaced, and every remaining video re-checked.

Some demos show the standard version, not your version. Where a video demonstrates something your hard limits rule out — a heel drop off a step, a supinated chin-up grip, a full-range dorsiflexion pull — the exercise card carries a Modified for you note saying exactly what to do differently. Follow the card, not the video.
